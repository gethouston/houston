It's the most common first message to an AI agent: one sentence holding a whole dream. *"GTA 6 but multiplayer." "An app that runs my whole company." "Airbnb for dogs, with payments and chat."*

The agent will try. It might even produce something. But it's almost never what you wanted, and when it breaks, nobody can tell why.

## Why giant requests fail

It isn't that the AI is dumb. Giant requests fail for **anyone**, including a team of senior engineers:

- **You can't test it.** If a thousand things changed, you can't check them all, so you don't really know what works.
- **You can't tell what broke.** When something goes wrong, the cause is hidden among a thousand pieces.
- **The agent has to guess.** A one-line dream leaves hundreds of decisions open. Each guess can be wrong, and the wrong guesses pile up.
- **You stop understanding your own product.** If you didn't watch it grow, you can't steer it.

## A slice

A **slice** is a small piece of your product that:

1. **works from start to finish.** A person can actually use it, even if it does very little;
2. **is small**, so Claude can build it in one go and you can check it in about five minutes;
3. **has a "done when":** a sentence anyone can check, without reading code.

| ❌ Not a slice | ✅ A slice |
|---|---|
| "The multiplayer system" | "A second player appears on the same street" |
| "The backend" | "A customer request I type in is still there after I refresh" |
| "Make the app nice" | "The request form fits on a phone screen, without zooming" |
| "Payments, chat and reviews" | "A dog owner can see a list of three sitters (fake data is fine)" |

Notice that slices are written as **what a person can do**, not as technical parts. That's on purpose: it's how you'll test them.

## Tell: slice your own idea

Write your idea in one sentence, big as it is. Then send this to Claude in a new session:

<PromptCard>
Here's my idea: [your idea, in one sentence]. Don't build anything yet. Help me slice it. First ask me up to 5 questions about who it's for and what problem it solves. Then propose: the smallest useful version, in one sentence, and 3 to 6 slices in order, each one something a person can do, with a "done when" I can check myself in five minutes, without reading code.
</PromptCard>

If you installed the tutor, `/academy:brief` does the same and saves the result as a one-page brief in your project.

No idea yet? Use one of these: *a booking page for a small business*, *a client portal where customers see the status of their order*, *a request tracker for your team*.

<RedFlag title="the agent builds 15 features in one go">
	You asked for slice 1 and Claude delivered a dashboard, settings, login, notifications and a dark mode, and half of them don't work. Don't try to fix it all. Say: *"Let's go back to just slice 1. Remove everything else, and tell me how to check that slice 1 works."* Small and working beats big and broken, every time.
</RedFlag>
