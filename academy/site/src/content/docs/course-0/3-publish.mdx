Your page works, but only on your computer. Let's see why, and fix it.

## The picture

<TwoComputers />

**Publishing** (also called **deploying**) means copying your files to a computer that never sleeps and is connected to the internet all the time. That computer is called a **server**, and companies that rent them are called **hosting** companies. Today you'll use one called Vercel. It's free for projects like this.

## Before you publish: check the folder

Everything in the folder becomes public. Ask Claude to look first:

<PromptCard>
I'm about to publish this folder to the internet. Look at every file in it: is there anything I shouldn't make public? Don't change anything, just tell me.
</PromptCard>

If you installed the tutor, you'll also see `CLAUDE.md` and a `.academy` folder. They're notes for your tutor (your language, your course), with nothing private in them, so it's fine to publish them.

## Publish

<Steps>

1. Go to [vercel.com/drop](https://vercel.com/drop) and create a free account if you don't have one (with your email or Google).

2. **Drag your `my-corner` folder** onto the page.

3. Choose a project name, for example `my-corner`, and click **Deploy**.

4. Wait a few seconds. Vercel gives you an address ending in `.vercel.app`. Click it: that's your page, on the internet.

5. **Open the same address on your phone.** Send it to a friend too.

</Steps>

:::note[Changing it later]
If you change your page now, drag the folder again. It goes up as a new copy with a new address. In Course 4 you'll connect your project so every change is published automatically.
:::

## What just happened

You copied one file, `index.html`, from your computer to Vercel's computer. When someone opens your address, their phone asks Vercel's computer for the file, and Vercel sends it. Your laptop isn't involved anymore: close it, and the page stays up.

<RedFlag title="publishing something private">
	Everything in a published folder can be seen by anyone. Never publish a folder with passwords, keys, personal documents or customer data in it. That's why you asked Claude to check first. Later in the program, private things get a safe place of their own.
</RedFlag>
