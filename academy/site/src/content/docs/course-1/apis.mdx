You'll hear "API" in almost every conversation about software. It sounds technical, but the idea is from everyday life.

## The picture

<ApiWaiter />

**An API is a waiter between programs.** Your app doesn't walk into another company's kitchen. It orders from a menu, the waiter takes the order in, and the dish comes back out.

| Word | In the restaurant | In software |
|---|---|---|
| **API** | the waiter | the way one program asks another for something |
| **API docs** | the menu | the page that lists what you can ask for, and how |
| **Request** | your order | what your app asks for |
| **Response** | the dish | what comes back (data, or an error) |
| **Endpoint** | one item on the menu | one specific thing you can ask for, like "current weather" |

API stands for *Application Programming Interface*. You can forget that right away; nobody uses the long name.

## Spot it in the wild

APIs are behind most of what your phone does. Think of three apps you used today and guess which APIs they call. A few hints:

- A weather widget asks a **weather API**.
- "Log in with Google" asks **Google's login API**: *"is this really them?"*
- A delivery app shows a map from a **maps API** and charges your card through a **payments API**.
- An app with an AI assistant sends your message to an **AI API**, such as Claude's.
